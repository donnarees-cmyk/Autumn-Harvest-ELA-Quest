Text 1: The Harvest Jubilee at Sun Gold Farm

By Maya Lin • Realistic Fiction Narrative

[1] Every October, Sun Gold Farm transformed into a bustling festival of autumn colors. Twelve-year-old Ethan pulled on his favorite wool sweater, crisp morning air greeting him as he stepped onto the porch. Today was the annual Harvest Jubilee, and for the first time, Ethan had been entrusted with managing the farm’s cider-pressing booth.

[2] Ethan walked past the pumpkin patch, where oversized, radiant orange squashes lay resting on golden straw. Nearby, giant banners flapped softly in the cool breeze, inviting visitors to apple-picking trails and corn maze adventures. "Remember, Ethan," his grandmother said warmly, handing him an apron. "The secret to Sun Gold cider isn't just the sweet Honeycrisp apples—it’s the care and pride we put into every single batch."

[3] As visitors flooded through the farm gates, Ethan worked tirelessly. He carefully sorted crisp red apples, operated the traditional wooden press, and poured steaming mugs of spiced hot cider for smiling guests. The rich aroma of cinnamon and fresh fruit filled the autumn air. When his friends invited him to take a break and ride the festival haywagon, Ethan looked back at the long line of happy families waiting at his booth. He politely declined with a cheerful smile, continuing his work with steady dedication.

[4] By sunset, the festival lights glowed brightly against the twilight sky. Ethan’s grandmother approached, taking in the spotless counter and empty apple crates. "You handled the entire jubilee with incredible responsibility, Ethan," she said proudly. Ethan grinned, feeling a deep sense of accomplishment that was far sweeter than any glass of cider.

Text 2: The Chemistry of Changing Leaves

Informational Text • Botanical Science Digest

[1] During the autumn season, temperate forests experience a dramatic visual transformation. Green canopy foliage dissolves into vivid displays of amber, gold, and vibrant crimson. While many people believe that chilly temperatures directly cause leaves to change color, the true biological trigger is the decreasing amount of daily sunlight as Earth shifts toward winter.

[2] In spring and summer, leaves act as miniature energy factories. They produce energy through photosynthesis, using a chemical called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and gives leaves their bright green appearance. Because chlorophyll constantly breaks down under sunlight, plants continuously manufacture new supplies throughout the warm growing season.

[3] As daylight hours shorten in autumn, deciduous trees begin preparing for winter dormancy. The plant forms a protective layer of cells at the base of each leaf stem, blocking the flow of nutrients. Consequently, chlorophyll production slows down and eventually halts. As existing chlorophyll fades away, pigments called carotenoids—which were hidden all summer—become visible, revealing bright yellow and orange hues. Simultaneously, trapped sugars in some leaves produce anthocyanin pigments, creating brilliant red and purple leaves. Far from a random decay, leaf color changes represent a remarkably organized process designed to protect tree health.
